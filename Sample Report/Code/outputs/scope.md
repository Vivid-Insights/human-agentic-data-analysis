# Analysis Scope — 2007 to 2023

Generated by `Code/02_select_scope.py`. Selection plus one derived column; nothing cleaned, altered or imputed.

## Decision

Keep `2007 <= year <= 2023`.

Reason: 2005 holds 27 countries and exactly one row with every column present, and 2006 holds 89 countries at 83% completeness — the weakest of any usable year. From 2007 the panel runs at 102 or more countries and 86–93% completeness every year. This keeps 17 consecutive years without carrying the two thin ones.

- Years: 17 consecutive, 2007–2023, no gaps.

## Balanced-panel options inside the scope

A balanced panel — the same countries in every year — is needed for some comparisons over time and not for others. The cost is country count.


| requirement                |   countries in all years |   country-years |
|:---------------------------|-------------------------:|----------------:|
| every column present       |                       40 |             680 |
| five key variables present |                       46 |             782 |

The 40 countries with every column present in all 17 years:

> Argentina, Bolivia, Cameroon, Chile, Colombia, Costa Rica, Denmark, Dominican Republic, Ecuador, El Salvador, Georgia, Ghana, India, Indonesia, Israel, Italy, Kazakhstan, Kenya, Kyrgyzstan, Lithuania, Mexico, Moldova, Nepal, Nicaragua, Pakistan, Peru, Philippines, Russia, Senegal, South Africa, Spain, Sweden, Tanzania, Thailand, Türkiye, Uganda, Ukraine, United Kingdom, Uruguay, Zimbabwe

That subset is not obviously skewed: in 2023 its median GDP per capita is $13,324 against $15,536 for the excluded countries, and its median life ladder 5.91 against 5.80. It does span rich and poor.

It does, however, exclude several of the largest economies — United States, China, Germany, France, Japan, Brazil, Canada, Australia, Nigeria — each of which is missing at least one value in at least one year. A strictly balanced panel would leave them out, which is worth weighing against whatever it buys.

Columns missing in **every** year a country appears — nothing within that country to interpolate from:

- `Log GDP per capita`: Somaliland region, South Sudan

Rows with a gap are not poorer or unhappier than complete rows — median life ladder 5.50 against 5.44, median GDP $13,974 against $13,470. That is reassuring about income, but says nothing about the mechanism: the corruption questions were not asked in several states where asking would be politically sensitive, which is missingness caused by the subject itself.

Note also that `Healthy life expectancy at birth` is interpolated and extrapolated by WHR to cover years the WHO series does not reach, so part of that column is already model output rather than measurement.

**Handling, decided 2026-09-07: no imputation.** Values are left missing. Each analysis uses whatever rows carry the variables it needs, on an unbalanced panel — a balanced panel is not required. Where a variable is systematically absent, the excluded countries are named and the likely bias stated, rather than a number being invented for them.
