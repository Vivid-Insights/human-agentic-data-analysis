# Cleaning

Generated by `Code/03_clean.py`. Cleaning here means **setting known-wrong values to missing**. Nothing is imputed, estimated or overwritten.

## Rules applied

### Haiti — `Healthy life expectancy at birth`

- **Years:** 2008, 2010, 2011, 2012, 2013, 2014
- **Decided:** 2026-09-07 (extended from [2008, 2010, 2011, 2012] the same day: the first version blanked only values below 40 years, which left 2013 (43.96) and 2014 (49.28) on the same fabricated ramp)
- **Reason:** The whole span is a broken backwards extrapolation. From 2010 to 2015 the series rises in exact steps of +5.32 years per year (17.36, 28.00, 33.32, 38.64, 43.96, 49.28, 54.60), reaching 54.60 in 2015, after which the steps become +0.30. A constant increment is what linear interpolation between two anchors produces, and the change of step locates the anchor: 2015 is observed, everything before it on the ramp is constructed. Haiti's true healthy life expectancy over this period is around 55 years, so the early values are also implausible on their own terms. 2015 onwards is kept.

- **Effect:** 6 value(s) blanked across 6 row(s).

### Venezuela — `Log GDP per capita`, `GDP per capita (PPP 2017 int$)`

- **Years:** every year in scope
- **Decided:** 2026-09-07
- **Reason:** The series falls from $19,197 in 2012 to $251 in 2019, a cumulative 98.7% collapse, ending far below the poorest country in the panel (Niger at $1,217 in the same year, Burundi at $740 at its lowest). Venezuela's real contraction was severe but nearer 75-80%. WHR takes Venezuelan GDP from the Penn World Table rather than the World Bank; Venezuela stopped publishing national accounts around 2014 and then experienced hyperinflation, so any price-based conversion explodes. The divergence grows from about 2014 with no clean breakpoint visible inside the file, so the whole series is blanked rather than a cut-off being guessed. Only GDP is affected - the survey-based measures are unaffected and are kept.

- **Effect:** 24 value(s) blanked across 12 row(s); a further 8 targeted cell(s) were already missing.

## Known problems deliberately left in place

- **2023 GDP is partly forecast.** The WHR 2024 appendix states 2023 GDP per capita was unavailable when the file was built and was extended from 2022 using OECD and World Bank growth forecasts. Accepted as-is (decided 2026-09-07): a forecast one year ahead is a reasonable estimate, and dropping 2023 would cost the most recent year of the panel. Any result that leans on 2023 GDP should say so.
- **`Healthy life expectancy at birth` is interpolated throughout.** WHR extends the WHO series to cover the sample period, so this column is part measurement and part model output for every country, not only Haiti.
